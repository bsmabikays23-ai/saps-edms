Statutory Framework Governing This System

SAPS National Instruction 3 of 2011 Mandates the mandatory registration of every reported crime. Officers are strictly prohibited from unlawfully refusing to open a docket or from discouraging a complainant from laying a charge. Every complaint presented at a police station must be recorded and issued a case reference number.
Section 205 — Constitution of the Republic of South Africa (Act 108 of 1996) Establishes the constitutional mandate of the South African Police Service to prevent, combat and investigate crime; to maintain public order; to protect and secure the inhabitants of the Republic and their property; and to uphold and enforce the law.
Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) 4 of 2013 Regulates the lawful collection, handling, processing and protection of personal data — including confidential victim statements. Personal information on this system is processed strictly for lawful policing purposes, with minimality, confidentiality and data-subject rights preserved under POPIA.
Cybercrimes Act 19 of 2020 — Warning Establishes severe legal penalties for unauthorised system access, data tampering, interception, interference or unauthorised disclosure of official government records. Offenders are liable for prosecution and penalties including imprisonment.
Victim Empowerment Programme & SAPS Service Charter Outlines the statutory rights of complainants, including the right to be treated with dignity and respect; to receive an official case reference number; to be kept informed of investigation progress; to be protected from secondary victimisation; and to access victim-support services.
Complainant Rights — Service Charter Summary
  • Right to a case reference (CAS) number.
  • Right to be informed of case progression.
  • Right to professional, impartial service.
  • Right to confidentiality of personal information.
  • Right to be treated with dignity at all times.
